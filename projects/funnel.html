Funnel & Data Analysis

Understanding where and why users drop off — and translating data into meaningful UX improvements that support better decision-making.

Role: UX & Data Analyst
Project: Personal Project
Duration: 2 weeks
Tools: Python · SQL · Data Visualization

Overview

This project explores how users move through a product funnel and where friction interrupts their journey. While conversion metrics can highlight *what* is happening, the goal here was to understand *why* users disengage and how design decisions can address these moments.

By combining quantitative analysis with UX thinking, I translated behavioral patterns into actionable insights that can inform more intuitive and supportive user experiences.

Goal

The objective was to identify key drop-off points within the funnel and uncover underlying factors influencing user decisions, with the aim of improving overall conversion and experience quality.

My Contribution

I conducted the full analysis independently — from data analysis using SQL and visualization in Python to interpreting patterns and framing them as UX opportunities. The focus was not only on identifying problems, but on understanding user intent and decision-making behavior.

Funnel Analysis

The funnel visualization provided a clear overview of how users progressed through the experience and where engagement significantly dropped.

A significant drop-off occurs in the middle of the funnel, suggesting that users encounter uncertainty or friction at the decision stage rather than at entry or completion.

This pattern indicates that users are initially interested, but something in the process disrupts their confidence or clarity before they commit. This is often a signal of missing information, unclear value, or cognitive overload.

Data Insights

To better understand the factors influencing user decisions, additional data dimensions were explored.

Average Price by Brand

Price distribution across brands revealed noticeable inconsistencies, which can introduce hesitation during decision-making. When users are unable to quickly evaluate value, they are more likely to abandon the process.

Top Products

The most popular products highlight clear user preferences. However, if these items are not prominently surfaced or contextualized, users may struggle to identify the “best” option, increasing cognitive effort.

Together, these insights suggest that decision complexity — rather than lack of interest — plays a key role in user drop-off.

UX Implications

The findings point to a fundamental UX challenge: users need support not just in navigating the interface, but in making confident decisions.

Simplifying the funnel alone is not sufficient — the experience must also reduce uncertainty and guide users toward meaningful choices. This can be achieved through clearer value communication, better product comparison, and strategic emphasis on relevant options.

In particular, improving price transparency and highlighting top-performing products can help users orient themselves more quickly and reduce the mental effort required to proceed.

Outcome

The project resulted in a clear understanding of where users disengage and why. More importantly, it demonstrates how data can be translated into UX decisions that directly impact user experience and business outcomes.

These insights provide a strong foundation for future iterations, including A/B testing and more targeted design interventions aimed at improving conversion.
